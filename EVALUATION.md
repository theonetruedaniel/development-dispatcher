and production admission are separate states. More researched repositories do
not imply more working integrations.

## Why start with rules

Rules provide a deterministic baseline that can be inspected, tested offline
and run without an extra model. A learned selector could help classify more
ambiguous work, but introduces quality, memory, latency and supervision costs.
The existing failed Laya qualification is a reason to retain the baseline until
better evidence exists, not a reason to hide or lower the thresholds.

The development controller has Codex presets and qualified Claude review candidates. The future Abrams
product router remains provider-neutral across separately admitted cloud and
local routes. This export does not choose its production runtime or model.

## Comparison protocol before claiming improvement

Use the already-approved task corpus and gates for the relevant comparison.
Keep the rules baseline, freeze exact inputs/versions, measure independently
verified task completion and missed escalations, and record total latency,
memory and accounted cost. Include failures and unavailable candidates.
Compare paired tasks before attributing a benefit to the selector.

Offline unit tests verify controller behavior. They cannot prove model quality,
host containment, production reliability or business ROI. No bake-off outcome
is manufactured by this demo, and delivery deadlines change no phase gate.
